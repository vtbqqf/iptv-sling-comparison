# IPTV vs Sling TV: Flexible Pricing, Multi-Connection Options

When people search for **IPTV vs Sling TV**, they are usually trying to answer a very practical question: how much TV can I get for the money, and will it actually fit the way I watch?

That sounds simple until you open the pricing pages. Then the math starts doing its little comedy routine.

The first thing to clear up is that **IPTV is a delivery method**, while **Sling TV is a specific live-TV streaming service**. So this comparison is really about a service such as TiviLive versus Sling TV, rather than IPTV as one single product.

TiviLive currently advertises **28,000+ live channels, 100,000+ VOD titles, EPG, sports and PPV content, and 4K/UHD/FHD playback**, with plans based on subscription length and connection count. Its published entry price is $15 for one month and one connection.

Sling takes a different approach. Instead of a huge channel-count headline, it sells defined packages such as Select, Orange, Blue, and Orange & Blue, then lets customers add extra channel bundles.

So when comparing **IPTV vs Sling TV**, price is only the first part of the story.

## IPTV vs Sling TV: What Is Actually Different?

The biggest difference is the shape of the service.

TiviLive presents itself as an IPTV subscription with a very broad catalog. Its pricing page says every plan includes the same core features, while customers choose the number of connections and how long they want to subscribe.

Sling is more modular. You start with a base package and can add extras. Sling Orange currently includes 30+ live channels and focuses heavily on sports, entertainment and family programming, including ESPN, Disney Channel and CNN. Sling Blue offers 40+ live channels and includes channels such as FOX News, CNN, Bravo and NFL Network.

That creates two very different shopping experiences.

With a broad IPTV package, the question is usually, “How much access do I get?”

With Sling, the question is more often, “Which exact channels do I need?”

Neither question is complicated. The annoying part is that they lead to very different subscription decisions.

## IPTV vs Sling TV for Sports

Sports is where this comparison gets interesting.

TiviLive advertises sports and PPV events as part of its plans, along with its large overall live-channel catalog.

Sling takes a more channel-specific approach. Orange includes ESPN, ESPN2 and TNT, while Blue carries sports channels such as FS1 and NFL Network. Sling's Sports Extra packages add networks such as NFL RedZone, Big Ten Network, SEC Network, ACC Network and Golf Channel depending on the base plan.

There is another detail sports viewers should not ignore: Sling notes that certain local broadcasts depend on the viewer's market, and sports programming can be subject to blackout restrictions.

That means the smart move is not simply asking, “Which service has more sports?”

Ask instead, “Does it carry the exact league, network and game I watch?”

That question saves a surprising amount of money.

## Which One Makes More Sense for Different Viewing Habits?

Someone who mainly wants a **defined set of mainstream US channels** may find Sling's package structure easier to understand. Sling lets viewers pick a base service such as Select, Orange or Blue, then add extras instead of paying for a giant all-in-one catalog.

Someone who is more interested in a **broad IPTV-style catalog, VOD access and multiple connection options** may be more interested in TiviLive's published plan structure. TiviLive says all its plans include its live-channel catalog, VOD library, EPG, sports/PPV content and support, with the main differences being duration and connection count.

The funny part is that both services are trying to solve the same household problem: nobody wants to pay for twenty things they never watch.

They just solve it in opposite directions.

Sling narrows the package and lets you build outward.

TiviLive starts broad and lets you choose how long you want access and how many connections you need.
